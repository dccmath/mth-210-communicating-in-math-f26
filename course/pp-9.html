    Section Portfolio Problem 9

    If you intend to try for “excellent” on this portfolio, see the instructions and ask Dr. Clark right away.
    🔗

    Conjecture 17.

    Let \(b : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) be defined by
    \begin{equation*} b(x) = \begin{cases} 2x+1 & \text{ if } x \geq 0\\ 2x & \text{ if } x \lt 0 \end{cases} \end{equation*}
    for all \(x \in \mathbb{R}\text{.}\) Then \(b\) is a bijection.
    🔗
    🔗
    Special instructions: If you choose this conjecture and it is false, then determine if \(b\) is an injection or a surjection. State a new theorem including only the true items and prove it. Separately -- outside of the proof -- provide one clear counterexample to any parts that are false.
    🔗

    Conjecture 18.

    Let \(f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) be defined by \(f(x) = (x+2)^{2} + 7\) for all \(x \in \mathbb{R}\text{.}\) Then \(f\) is a bijection.
    🔗
    🔗
    Special instructions: As written, this function is neither a surjection nor an injection. If you choose this, you must first include concrete counterexamples to show why it is not injective and not surjective. Then change both the domain and codomain to the largest possible sets that make \(f\) a bijection. State your new result with a properly written theorem statement that includes the corrected domain and codomain, and prove the new statement.
    🔗

    Conjecture 19.

    Let \(M\) be the set of all \(2 \times 2\) matrices with real number entries. The function \(tr: M \to M\) defined by the rule
    \begin{equation*} tr \left( \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}\right) = \begin{bmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{bmatrix} \text{ for all matrices in } M \end{equation*}
    is a bijection. (Notice that \(tr\) makes a very small but important change to where \(b\) and \(c\) are located.)
    🔗
    🔗
    Special instructions: If you choose this conjecture and it is false, then determine if \(tr\) is an injection or is a surjection. State a new theorem including only the true items and prove it. Separately -- outside of the proof -- provide one clear counterexample to any parts that are false.
    🔗

    Subsection Instructions for the draft

    Choose only one of the conjectures and address it. Ignore the other ones.
    🔗
    Upload a scanned image of your draft to the Blackboard assignment.
    🔗
    To earn math successful, your draft must address only one conjecture:
    🔗
    1. Each conjecture asks you to prove that a function is a bijection. Be sure to follow the special instructions on what to do if the conjecture is false -- it’s different in each case.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. This time, you do not need to include any other examples of the theorems in action (other than counterexamples).
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Instructions for the writing revision

    Submit a PDF of your writing revision to the Blackboard assignment.
    🔗
    To earn math & writing successful, your writing revision must do all of the following:
    🔗
    1. Follow all instructions for proofs, examples, and counterexamples from the draft. Each of these should now be formatted following the writing conventions.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Address all feedback from the draft.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Type your work in LaTeX. Use the template, how-to guide, and sample portfolio solution.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Include a “view only” link to your Overleaf document in the Blackboard submission’s text field.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Follow all of the conventions listed below (click each one to get more details and examples):
      🔗
      🔗
    Writing convention 1 Include a true and correctly worded theorem statement just before the proof
    🔗
    Writing convention 2 At the very beginning of the proof, state all assumptions and explain what will be proved
    🔗
    Writing convention 3 State the proof method being used and any additional information required to clarify it
    🔗
    Writing convention 4 Give clear, correct, and organized justification for each statement, and have no important errors or omissions
    🔗
    Writing convention 5 Define all variables and new notation before they are used
    🔗
    Writing convention 6 Cite definitions clearly and correctly each time one is used
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Instructions for excellence

    To earn math & writing excellent, thoroughly address all comments from the draft and writing revision, and thoroughly attend to details when you do so. Then do this:
    1. Ask Dr. Clark for a personalized challenge.
      This is the challenge round! Ask Dr. Clark for a specific challenge about how to earn “excellent” on this portfolio. Ask earlier rather than later, since it might involve making a different choice of conjecture.
