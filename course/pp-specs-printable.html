    Handout Writing Conventions for MTH 210
    

    Required writing conventions.

    These are writing conventions that we will learn and use in MTH 210. Every writing revision and final revision of each portfolio should follow all of these.
    🔗
    See the full document on Blackboard for detailed explanations and examples.
    🔗
    To earn “math & writing successful” or better, a proof must do all of these:
    🔗
    Writing convention 1 Include a true and correctly worded theorem statement just before the proof
    🔗
    Writing convention 2 At the very beginning of the proof, state all assumptions and explain what will be proved
    🔗
    Writing convention 3 State the proof method being used and any additional information required to clarify it
    🔗
    Writing convention 4 Give clear, correct, and organized justification for each statement, and have no important errors or omissions
    🔗
    Writing convention 5 Define all variables and new notation before they are used
    🔗
    Writing convention 6 Cite definitions clearly and correctly each time one is used
    🔗
    🔗

    Additional writing conventions.

    Every proof must do most of these, where “most” means occasional errors in up to 3 items, or consistent errors in 1 item.
    🔗
    One way to earn “excellent” is for a proof to do all of these thoroughly and with no noticeable omissions.
    🔗
    Writing convention 7 Be typed in a new Overleaf document
    🔗
    Writing convention 8 Follow all basic WRT 150 writing requirements, including spelling, grammar, and punctuation, and avoiding shorthand
    🔗
    Writing convention 9 Use “we” instead of “I” or “you”
    🔗
    Writing convention 10 Begin every sentence with a word, not a symbol
    🔗
    Writing convention 11 Include every equation or formula in a sentence, including correct punctuation
    🔗
    Writing convention 12 Display (center) important equations and align “\(=\)” signs
    🔗
    Writing convention 13 Use appropriate symbols in formulas; avoid excessive symbols (or writing out formulas in words) otherwise
    🔗
    Writing convention 14 Use equation numbers sparingly and always refer to them in the text
    🔗
    Writing convention 15 Write exponents, fractions, operations, and all other mathematical symbols in professional textbook format
    🔗
    Writing convention 16 Write all variables in italics
    🔗
    Writing convention 17 Include a summary at the end of the proof and an “end of proof” symbol
    🔗
