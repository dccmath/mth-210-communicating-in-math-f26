    Section Portfolio Problem 8

    Conjecture 15.

    Let \(A\) be the set of all integers \(n\) for which 4 divides \(n-7\text{,}\) and let \(B\) be the set of all integers that are congruent to 3 (mod 12). Then \(A = B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Special instructions: If you choose this conjecture, provide examples of at least 5 numbers form each set in roster notation, and show briefly how you know that number is in that set. Then show thaty our conclusion is true.
    🔗

    Conjecture 16.

    If \(A\) and \(B\) are subsets of some universal set \(U\text{,}\) then \((A \cup B) - (A \cap B) = (A - B) \cup (B - A)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Special instructions: Choose this conjecture if you want to aim for “excellent”. If you choose this conjecture, provide an example of both sets \(A\) and \(B\) with at least 5 numbers in each. Then show how to calculate the sets on either side of the equation and show that the results agree with your conclusion. Read the “Instructions for excellence” carefully if that is your goal.
    🔗

    Subsection Instructions for the draft

    Choose only one of the conjectures and address it. Ignore the other one.
    🔗
    You might want to look at the “Instructions for excellence” below before choosing -- that makes a difference this time!
    🔗
    Upload a scanned image of your draft to the Blackboard assignment.
    🔗
    To earn math successful, your draft must address only one conjecture:
    🔗
    1. If your conjecture is true:
      🔗
      • State it as a theorem and write a proof of it in know-show format.
        🔗
        🔗
      • Follow the special instructions written underneath your conjecture.
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
    2. If your conjecture is false:
      🔗
      • Determine if one of the sets is a subset of the other set. If so, state an appropriate theorem about subsets and prove it.
        🔗
        🔗
      • Separately, before the proof, provide one clear counterexample that shows that the other subset statement is false.
        🔗
        🔗
      • Follow the special instructions written underneath your conjecture.
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Instructions for the writing revision

    First, wait for draft feedback on Blackboard!
    🔗
    Then, submit a PDF of your writing revision to the Blackboard assignment.
    🔗
    To earn math & writing successful, your writing revision must do all of the following:
    🔗
    1. Follow all instructions for proofs, examples, and counterexamples from the draft. Each of these should now be formatted following the writing conventions.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Address all feedback from the draft.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Type your work in LaTeX. Use the template, how-to guide, and sample portfolio solution.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Include a “view only” link to your Overleaf document in the Blackboard submission’s text field.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Follow all of the conventions listed below (click each one to get more details and examples):
      🔗
      🔗
    Writing convention 1 Include a true and correctly worded theorem statement just before the proof
    🔗
    Writing convention 2 At the very beginning of the proof, state all assumptions and explain what will be proved
    🔗
    Writing convention 3 State the proof method being used and any additional information required to clarify it
    🔗
    Writing convention 4 Give clear, correct, and organized justification for each statement, and have no important errors or omissions
    🔗
    Writing convention 5 Define all variables and new notation before they are used
    🔗
    Writing convention 6 Cite definitions clearly and correctly each time one is used
    🔗
    Also follow most of the Additional writing conventions (open the link to see what “most” means).
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Instructions for excellence

    To earn math & writing excellent, thoroughly address all comments from the draft and writing revision, and thoroughly attend to detail when you do so. Then do the following:
    1. Choose Conjecture 16 and follow these additional instructions.
      Choose this (more difficult) conjecture, then also write your proof using only “set algebra” as we have discussed in class. In particular, do not use element-chasing. Your proof should be clearly and thoroughly explained, both in words and in symbols, as usual. (This is in addition to following all instructions for the writing revision -- in particular, your writing revision must include all required proofs, examples, and disproofs.)
      🔗
      🔗
    Note that “excellent” is not automatic upon revision, and relies on showing thorough attention to these instructions.
