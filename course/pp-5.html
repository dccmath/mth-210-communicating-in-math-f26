    Section Portfolio Problem 5 - Team project

    Theorem 8.

    For each integer \(a\text{,}\) if \(7 \nmid a\) then \(a \equiv 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, \text{ or } 6 \pmod{7}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Hint 1.
    What does the Division Algorithm tell you about \(a\) in this situation?
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 9.

    For each integer \(a\text{,}\) if \(7 \mid a^{2}\) then \(7 \mid a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Hint 2.
    The previous theorem will be helpful -- be clear and precise about how you use it. Here’s a handy template: “Because (statement you know is true), by Theorem 8, (conclusion from the theorem).”
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 10.

    The real number \(\sqrt{7}\) is irrational.
    🔗
    🔗
    Hint 3.
    We completed a similar proof in Section 3.3. Feel free to use it as a starting point -- but be very careful to understand where this proof must be different! Look for ways to use Theorem 9 in the proof of Theorem 10.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Instructions for the draft

    To earn math successful, your group must do all of the following:
    🔗
    1. Work together as a team to find a proof for all three theorems. Know-show tables are fine.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Put them all in one document, but write a separate theorem statement and proof for each.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Include at least two examples that illustrate Theorem 8, and two more for Theorem 9. You don’t need examples for Theorem 10. Put the examples outside of the proofs (see the sample portfolio solution for an example of how to format these).
      🔗
      🔗
    4. One group member should submit a scanned image of your draft to the Blackboard assignment.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Instructions for the writing revision

    First, wait for draft feedback on Blackboard!
    🔗
    To earn math & writing successful, you must do all of the following:
    🔗
    Individually, submit your project reflection (link is on Blackboard). This must be completed in order to earn your individual portfolio grade.
    🔗
    As a group, do all of these:
    🔗
    1. Include all proofs and examples required, as in the draft.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Address all feedback from the draft.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Type your work in LaTeX. Use the template, how-to guide, and sample portfolio solution.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Do not include a “view only” because Dr. Clark will share an Overleaf document with your team (so I’ll already have access to it).
      🔗
      🔗
    5. One group member should submit a PDF of your writing revision to the Blackboard assignment.
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Follow all of the conventions listed below (click each one to get more details and examples):
      🔗
      🔗
    Writing convention 1 Include a true and correctly worded theorem statement just before the proof
    🔗
    Writing convention 2 At the very beginning of the proof, state all assumptions and explain what will be proved
    🔗
    Writing convention 3 State the proof method being used and any additional information required to clarify it
    🔗
    Writing convention 4 Give clear, correct, and organized justification for each statement, and have no important errors or omissions
    🔗
    Writing convention 5 Define all variables and new notation before they are used
    🔗
    Writing convention 6 Cite definitions clearly and correctly each time one is used
    🔗
    Also follow most of the Additional writing conventions (open the link to see what “most” means).
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Instructions for excellence

    To earn math & writing excellent, thoroughly address all comments from the draft and writing revision, and thoroughly attend to detail when you do so. Then do the following:
    1. Polish.
      Polish your writing so that it follows all of the Additional writing conventions thoroughly, with no noticeable omissions. Click or tap the link to see all of them.
      🔗
      🔗
    Note that “excellent” is not automatic upon revision, and relies on showing thorough attention to detail.
